# Niyora — Emotion regulation content bank (v1, research-backed)

Source science for the leveled emotion-training game. Five emotions:
**irritability, sadness, anger, anxiety, overwhelmed.** Each maps to game levels
(education → strategy/scenario → interpersonal → reflect). Built from a
fact-checked deep-research pass (25 sources, 3-vote adversarial verification).

## How to read this doc

Every claim carries a confidence tag. Ship copy only from **[VERIFIED]** and
**[MEDIUM]**. Never ship **[DO NOT USE]**. **[GAP]** = we want this but it did
not clear verification in this pass; needs a second research pass before it
becomes in-app copy.

- **[VERIFIED]** — 3/0 adversarial vote, primary/meta-analytic source.
- **[MEDIUM]** — confirmed but single study or correlational; state softly.
- **[GAP]** — named in the design but unsourced/unverified here. Do not ship yet.
- **[DO NOT USE]** — actively refuted (0/3). A plausible myth; safe only as a
  *wrong* quiz option, never as a fact.

Governing tone rule (from `DESIGN.md` + the dashboard plan): the science
*normalizes and explains*, it never pathologizes. "Your feelings are real, just
louder" — never "something is wrong with you."

## Cross-cutting foundations (apply to every emotion)

These three are the spine. They recur across all five tracks.

### F1. The master fit/backfire card — reappraisal vs distraction by intensity
**[VERIFIED]** At **low** emotional intensity, engaging with the feeling and
*reframing* it (cognitive reappraisal) fits and helps. At **high** intensity,
people shift to *distraction*, and that is the adaptive move — because distraction
blocks the emotional information early, "before it gathers force," whereas
reappraisal requires engaging with content you can't think clearly about once
you're flooded. Replicated across 3 experiments (disturbing images, unpredictable
shocks) and a ~51-study meta-analysis.
- Fit: reframe when mildly bothered; distract/step-away when highly activated.
- Backfire: trying to reframe mid-flood fails and can make it worse.
- This is the engine's core rule: **the same strategy is right or wrong depending
  on her intensity.** That is what keeps the game non-obvious.
- Source: Sheppes et al. 2011, *Psychological Science*
  (journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797611418350); Sheppes & Levin 2013
  (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3647109/).
- Caveat: a 2024 study suggests the effect may be emotion-specific (stronger for
  disgust than fear) — direction holds, generality narrows.

### F2. Self-compassion — the foundation layer
**[VERIFIED]** Three components: **self-kindness** (vs harsh self-criticism),
**common humanity** (vs isolation), **mindfulness** (balanced awareness vs
over-identifying). Robustly linked to lower depression (r ≈ −.51) and anxiety
(r ≈ −.65); RCTs show medium-to-large drops in depression/anxiety/distress
(Kirby 2017: depression d=0.64, anxiety d=0.49).
- Myth-buster **[VERIFIED]**: "self-compassion makes you soft/unmotivated" is
  false — it's tied to *higher* self-efficacy (r ≈ .35), mastery goals, and *less*
  procrastination.
- Source: Neff 2003 (self-compassion.org/.../empirical.article.pdf); Neff 2023,
  *Annual Review of Psychology* (self-compassion.org/.../PsychReviewInPress.pdf).
- Caveat: mostly cross-sectional; intervention effects shrink vs active controls
  (depression survives, some anxiety effects don't). State as "linked to," not
  "causes."

### F3. The PMS lens — mechanism + prevalence (the education spine)
- **[VERIFIED]** Premenstrual mood symptoms come from altered brain *sensitivity
  to normal hormone fluctuation*, **not abnormal hormone levels**. Women with PMDD
  can't be told apart from others by their blood hormone levels. Involves GABA-A
  receptor sensitivity to allopregnanolone (a progesterone metabolite).
  Source: Hantsoo & Payne (PMC7231988); 2023 Frontiers review (PMC10017536).
  *Caveat: the precise molecular model is the authors' hypothesis — don't overstate
  the neurobiology.*
- **[VERIFIED]** It's the **rate of change** in allopregnanolone across the luteal
  phase, not the absolute level, that triggers symptoms (rodent rapid-withdrawal
  studies). Source: PMC7231988.
- **[VERIFIED]** PMS is common: pooled worldwide prevalence ~**47.8%** ("about
  half"), ~**20%** severe enough to disrupt daily life. Source: StatPearls
  (NBK560698). *Caveat: huge cross-country spread (12–98%) — say "about half," not
  a precise number.*
- **[VERIFIED]** Clinically confirmed PMDD is **~3.2%** (prospective) vs ~7.7% by
  self-report — self-report **overstates** it. Source: Reilly/Gao et al. 2024,
  *J. Affective Disorders* (S0165032724000764). *Great myth/fact item: true PMDD
  is rarer than the internet suggests.*
- **[MEDIUM]** Severity is driven by an estrogen×progesterone *interaction*, not
  absolute levels (single prospective study). Source: Nevatte et al. 2019
  (PMC6888463).

## 1 · Irritability

- **Mechanism / PMS lens:** F3. The premenstrual window is when normal hormone
  *swings* amplify reactivity — real, common, not a character flaw.
- **Physical drivers (HALT):** **[GAP]** The HALT model (hungry/angry/lonely/tired)
  and blood-sugar/hunger link were not verified this pass. Sleep→irritability has
  partial support: **[GAP/weak]** sleep debt reduces prefrontal control of the
  amygdala (PMC6122651, not in verified set); an N-of-1 study tied sleep loss to
  cravings (OR~30, PMC7151045 — too weak to cite). *Needs a second pass.*
- **Duration / time-course:** **[GAP]** A source noted PMDD symptoms peak ~3–4 days
  before menses to ~3 days after, absent the post-menstrual week — extracted but
  not in the verified set. Verify before shipping.
- **Prevalence / normalize:** F3 (PMS ~half; disruptive ~20%).
- **Strategies:** F1 is the key card here — at high irritability, *distract/step
  away*, don't try to reframe mid-spike. F2 (self-compassion) counters the
  "why am I like this" self-criticism.
- **Interpersonal:** **[GAP]** assertiveness/"I" statements unsourced this pass.
- **Myth quiz:** "PMS irritability means your hormones are abnormal" → **false**,
  it's *sensitivity to normal* fluctuation (F3).

## 2 · Anger

- **Mechanism:** shares F3 amplification premenstrually; anger as a
  high-intensity, high-arousal state → F1 says down-regulate before engaging.
- **Strategies:**
  - **[VERIFIED] The venting myth (headline card).** Hitting a pillow / "letting it
    out" while dwelling on the trigger **increases** anger and aggression; doing
    *nothing* (sitting quietly) reduced both more. Directly refutes catharsis
    theory. Source: Bushman 2002, *PSPB*. *Caveat: rumination-vs-distraction
    aggression gap wasn't itself significant — frame as "venting beats nothing at
    making you angrier."*
  - F1: at high anger, distraction/timeout > reframing.
  - **[GAP]** Gottman physiological self-soothing / 20-min timeout under "flooding"
    — a Gottman blog was found (secondary), no verified primary claim. Needs
    sourcing.
- **Interpersonal:** **[GAP]** the "assert the real grievance vs soothe" decision
  rule (assertiveness training, softened startup) — sources found, claims
  unverified. This is the ethically important one; prioritize in the next pass.
- **Myth quiz:** "Venting your anger lets off steam and calms you down" → **false**
  (Bushman). Strong, counterintuitive item.

## 3 · Sadness / low mood

- **Mechanism:** F3 premenstrual amplification; low mood pulls toward withdrawal.
- **Strategies:**
  - **[VERIFIED] Rumination worsens it (headline card).** Dwelling/"brooding"
    exacerbates depression, enhances negative thinking, impairs problem-solving,
    and erodes social support. Source: Nolen-Hoeksema, Wisco & Lyubomirsky 2008.
    *Caveat: targets maladaptive "brooding," distinct from healthy reflection.*
  - **[VERIFIED] Behavioral activation (the counter-move).** Doing small,
    meaningful actions is about as effective as full CBT for depression — it
    directly counters rumination's shutdown of mood-lifting behavior. Source:
    BJPsych Open 2022 (PMC9379999); Cuijpers meta-analyses.
  - F2 (self-compassion) especially load-bearing for low mood.
- **Interpersonal:** **[GAP]** reaching out vs isolating — implied by the social-
  support finding but no verified interpersonal-skill claim.
- **Myth quiz:** "When you're down, sit with it and think it through until it
  lifts" → **false** if it's brooding; *acting* (behavioral activation) beats
  ruminating.

## 4 · Anxiety

- **Mechanism:** F3; anxiety is the intensity axis F1 was literally tested on
  (unpredictable-shock paradigm).
- **Strategies:**
  - **[VERIFIED]** F1 is the core card: high anxiety → distraction blocks it early;
    low/anticipatory anxiety → reframing fits. The "why" (distraction intercepts
    before the feeling gathers force) is teachable. Source: Sheppes 2011/2013.
  - F2 (self-compassion) linked to markedly lower trait anxiety (r ≈ −.65).
  - **[GAP]** DBT TIPP / paced breathing / distress tolerance — named in the brief,
    no verified primary source this pass. (The app already has breathing content;
    the *claim* linking it to acute anxiety needs sourcing.)
- **Interpersonal:** **[GAP]**.
- **Myth quiz:** "You should always face anxiety head-on and reason it away" →
  **oversimplified** — at high intensity, distraction first is the evidence-based
  move (F1).

## 5 · Overwhelmed

- **Mechanism:** F3; overwhelm as a flooded, high-load state → F1 favors
  disengagement (distraction/step-away) before problem-solving.
- **Strategies:**
  - F1 + F2 apply.
  - **[GAP]** Cognitive offloading / chunking / externalizing the load — named in
    the brief, not verified this pass. Intuitive and likely sourceable; flag until
    then.
- **Interpersonal:** **[GAP]** asking for help / delegating.
- **Myth quiz:** **[GAP]** pending a verified offloading claim.

## DO NOT USE (refuted 0/3 — safe only as *wrong* quiz options)

- ✗ "A rapid drop in allopregnanolone lowers GABA-A sensitivity/chloride influx —
  that's *the* mechanism." Refuted as stated (PMC10017536). The *sensitivity*
  framing (F3) is right; this specific causal chain is not settled.
- ✗ "Women with PMDD have significantly lower estrogen than others." Refuted
  (PMC6888463).
- ✗ "Low estrogen makes the hypothalamus release norepinephrine, dropping
  serotonin/dopamine" — a tidy cascade, refuted (NBK560698).

These are *excellent* distractors precisely because they sound scientific.

## Gaps — second research pass needed before these ship

The following are required by the design but unverified here. Run a focused
follow-up (each needs a citable primary/clinical source):

1. **Interpersonal skills (highest priority):** DBT DEARMAN, assertiveness
   training, "I" statements, and the **assert-vs-soothe decision rule**. Sources
   surfaced (Speed et al. 2018; Gottman softened startup) but claims weren't
   verified. This is the ethical core (don't train self-silencing).
2. **DBT TIPP / distress tolerance** (acute anxiety, anger down-regulation).
3. **Gottman flooding + timeout / physiological self-soothing** (anger, conflict).
4. **ACT acceptance / cognitive defusion** ("let it drift").
5. **Cognitive offloading / chunking** (overwhelm).
6. **HALT + physical drivers** (hunger/blood sugar, sleep) with strong sources;
   and **episode duration** per emotion.

## Game mapping (bank field → level)

| Bank field | Level | Mechanic |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism + prevalence (F3) | L1 Education | 3-option "what's true" (myths as distractors) |
| Strategy cards w/ fit/backfire (F1, per-emotion) | L2 Scenario | match strategy to *her intensity*; wrong plays out |
| Interpersonal [GAP] | L3 Respond | assert-vs-soothe under load |
| — | L4 Reflect | "did it work last time?" → next / reach out |
| Self-compassion (F2) | woven through all | recurring foundation beat |

The **fit/backfire** columns (F1 especially) are what make L2 non-obvious: correct
depends on her stated intensity, so no answer is universally "the healthy one."
