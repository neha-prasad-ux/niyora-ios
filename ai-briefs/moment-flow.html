Acts and topics — how the two lists connect

Two lists described “what happens next” with no rule joining them: 13 acts (what she does) and 10 modules (what happened). Undefined handoffs get guessed — the training script had already invented one, quietly repointing the deleted deweight beat at self-compassion.

DECIDED: acts are the unit, topics are context.
The act set closes — an act can only aim at one of five targets (the other person · the situation · a third party · myself · my safety). Topics fan out forever; targets don't. A new situation needs new copy, never a new act. The other way round never finishes: a landlord, a sister-in-law, a diagnosis would each need their own module.

The 10 “modules” were a shortcut that hard-coded act+topic pairs. work-clash is really “say it to them, at work”. repair is “own my part, with a partner”. Cells in a grid, presented as a list.

Stream F builds 13 act templates plus this matrix, not 10 situation modules. One generator runs whatever she picks, on three inputs: the act (shape of the output), the topic (the words, what's at stake), and her own text (the facts, never invented).

The honest cost: hand-written modules produce better copy than a template filled at runtime, and 13 × 10 = 130 cells is exactly why the shortcut happened. How big that quality loss is has not been tested.
WE OFFER, SHE CHOOSES (Neha, 2026-07-25): “we should tell her the consequence, the outcome, she can choose to do it or not, its upto her. giving options is our role, chosing is hers.”

This removed a column I had drafted. I had marked shame → own my part and lonely → tell one person as never offer, because the evidence says both are weak for her. That was me deciding on her behalf. The fix is not to hide the option — it is to say the honest outcome next to it and let her weigh it.

Two reasons to withhold survive, and neither is paternalism:
1 · Suppressed for safety. When the DV screen fires we don't offer confrontational acts and we don't explain why. Not because we know better, but because we cannot see her situation and the option itself could get her hurt. A consequence line there would be us guessing at a risk we can't assess.
2 · Doesn't apply. “Say it to them” for a flat mood invents an antagonist. bias → own my part is the sharp one: bias is not a two-sided conflict, so owning part of it is factually wrong, not merely risky.

The column is now “suppressed / doesn't apply” — never “I decided against it”.

THE UNIVERSAL DV LINE — shown to everyone, not just when the screen fires.
Whenever A · say it to them is offered, it carries:

“worth knowing: confronting someone doesn't work when there's abuse in the picture. that's a different situation and it needs different help.”

Universal is the whole point, not a detail. Shown only on a screen hit, this line is a disclosure — it tells her the app has concluded she is being abused, on a device someone else may be reading. Shown to every woman, that inverts: no disclosure, no diagnosis, and it reaches the women detection misses — which matters, because the screen will fail on real cases.

It also asks her to assess nothing. That was the fatal flaw in “only if you feel safe with them” as a gate: coercive control distorts precisely that judgement, and a woman being controlled often does believe she is safe. A statement of fact needs no self-assessment at the moment she is least able to make one.

Both layers, independent. The line catches the women the screen misses. The suppression — A/B/C still not generated at all, no caveat, no explanation — catches the women who read the line and don't apply it to themselves, which in coercive relationships is common: recognising your own situation in a general statement is usually the last thing to happen, not the first.
Why numb changed. The row was take something off my plate · look after myself · let it be — three ways to do less. Behavioural activation says the flat need an act MOST, not least, so a menu of withdrawal is the wrong medicine for flatness.

This is the same mistake Neha caught earlier on “lift” vs “relax”: BA backs engaging acts, and borrowing its evidence for rest borrows the wrong finding. Twice in one night in the same direction — too protective — which is worth knowing about how this table was drafted.

Note this is the opposite of the comfort-act decision, where soothing was chosen deliberately. Different node, different job: the comfort act is for a woman who has declined to fix anything; the numb menu is for a woman still choosing.

Two rules cut across every row. M · get safe is gated and never a chip in any topic — a stop and a referral with no content of its own. And when the safety screen fires, A, B and C are not generated at all, which leaves sad and numb untouched but cuts real-hurt down to its low-effort option. That is correct, and it is also the uncomfortable part: our best-evidenced act is the one we withhold from the women in most danger.

What changed in this revision, and why

00d

Only 6 of the 13 acts have any evidence — and 7 have none at all

The act menu is the richest thing in the flow and the least evidenced. Graded honestly, and the ungraded ones are now marked no science line permitted.

The one strong act is say it to them (assertiveness g=0.62, N=503; self-guided ES≈1.00, best in the set) — but DESC, the technique we actually teach, has zero trials. It comes from a 1976 trade book. Claim assertiveness, never DESC.
“Hold a line” — “boundaries” has no trial evidence at all and “grey rock” has literally zero. Use the evidenced words instead: refusal skills, work-detachment (d=0.36).
“Own my part” — strong for the recipient, weak-to-null for her own wellbeing (nulls at N=172, N=462). Never claim it helps her.
Seven acts were never graded at all (ask for the thing · find out · get someone whose job it is · work out what I want · take something off my plate · look after myself · get safe). They exist in the taxonomy and in no evidence document. This matters because an agent told to “attach evidence honestly” to an ungraded act invents a rationale in good faith. The standing rule: shipping an unevidenced act is fine; attaching a science line to it is not.
00e

Two acts now carry a safety precondition

Tell one person → someone she feels safe with who will think it through with her. Say it to them → only if she'd feel safe saying it.

Why “safe space” alone selects the wrong listener: Nils & Rimé found recovery happened exclusively when the listener stimulated cognitive work. Purely empathic listening produced closeness, less loneliness, and an impression of feeling better with no actual recovery. “Someone who gives me a safe space” reads to most people as someone who'll take my side — precisely the listener who produced the impression and not the recovery. So: safe is the floor, thinks with you is what makes it work.
Why the second one is a layer and never the gate: when there are signs of coercive control the confrontational option is never generated at all. Asking “do you feel safe with him?” after showing “say it to him directly” is too late — the idea is already in the room. And coercive control distorts exactly that self-assessment, so her answer is the least reliable input at the precise moment we'd be relying on it. The screen decides whether she ever sees the option; her answer only lets her decline one we already allowed.
00f

All 19 why-lines are approved — and there is now a stopping rule

Neha wrote every one. The bar she set: educate AND motivate, or it's not approved. Then a second rule to make review terminate.

The stopping rule, in her words: “if there is no wrong science, fake, wrongful info we should chill. I will test more and get back, change if needed.” So the bar for shipping a line is not “is this the best possible sentence” — it is “is anything in it false, unsupported, or misleading?” Agents may reopen approved copy only to challenge a claim, never to improve its style. Without that, copy review never terminates.
What her drafts changed: she motivates by naming a payoff, where mine only informed — but the first versions promised outcomes we can't back. The fix that came out of it is the general rule now: when a line promises the wrong thing, point the promise at something true rather than deleting it. Four of her lines beat the spec's own copy, most notably the closing rating, where the spec's “and now?” educates nothing and would fail her own test.
00b

“Can you eat something?” is gone

Was: the body check asked about food and offered to eat now or save it for later. Now: not asked at all. The beat keeps sleep and movement.

Three problems, all closed by not asking. No evidence — the body-check finding covers sleep, stress and fatigue and never hunger; it was the one leg of this beat with nothing under it. No safe claim available — the only on-topic study (glucose and partner aggression) isn't in our bank, sits beside a literature that failed to replicate, and is unusable anyway because “blood sugar affects mood” is a physiology claim we've banned. An uncleared safety flag — our own audit records this as an unscreened food prompt, with binge eating elevated through the luteal phase. Asking a woman mid-distress about eating is not a neutral question, and no rewrite of the copy closes that.
Restoring it needs both the study verified and the screen designed. Not one or the other.
00c

The if-then is two slots, and the trigger comes from her own words

Was: “if this happens again i would ___” — one slot, generic trigger. Now: “if ___ happens, then i ___”, with the first blank pre-seeded from her own opening sentence, tense-shifted and editable.

Why two slots: the d=0.53 effect is for a specific trigger paired with a response, and the specificity is the part the mechanism depends on. “If this happens again” is precisely the version that doesn't carry it.
Why seed it from her sentence: for the model to offer “if he brings it up at dinner” it would have to invent a he and a dinner — the invented-person bug, in the highest-stakes slot in the flow. Seeding from what she already wrote makes the facts correct by construction. She fills both halves; the model may offer options, never facts.
Honest note: the evidence is for plans people form themselves. Whether picking from options carries the same effect is not established.
NEW

Every button gets a line saying what it's for

We ask her to choose at 16 points and almost never say what the choice is for. She taps chips on trust. activity_context is the only beat in the whole flow whose job is to explain why an action helps — the other fifteen are bare.

Why this isn't cosmetic: if she only learns “the app told me to breathe”, she has an app habit. If she learns “waiting twenty minutes is what stops me sending the thing I'd take back”, she has a skill that survives the phone going back in her pocket. Transfer is the whole point, and transfer needs the reason stated.
The trap: “explain why” is exactly the prompt that tempts a mechanism claim, which the voice rules ban. So the line says what it does for her — ✅ “this is what stops you sending the thing you'd take back” — never ❌ “this lowers your physiological arousal”. Where the evidence is thin, say so: “that's just sensible, not science.”
Never on the safety beats. Crisis and the safe-check stay unexplained on purpose — an explanation there reads as persuasion, and we don't persuade her about her own safety. And it fades after about three exposures: teaching stops when she knows.
Two of the sixteen are model slots rather than fixed copy, because they have to name her actual situation: the three act options (she's choosing between a direct, a preparatory and a self-directed act with nothing telling her what separates them) and the if-then.
NEW

The tired question moved to where it can be answered

Was: the body check asked “now / tomorrow?”. Now: the body check only names the cost; the timing question is asked at time-it.

Why: at the body check she hasn't been regulated, hasn't seen the options, and hasn't chosen an act — so “now or tomorrow?” was asking her to time an action that didn't exist yet. Unanswerable. By time-it there's a specific thing to time: the message she just drafted. “do it now anyway” stays a real, un-shamed option either way.
00

The ending, and a number either side of it

Both branches now exit the same shape: something concrete → an if-then → we good? → a rating → [human nudge] → close. She picks an act and the if-then is for doing it; she picks “none feel possible” and it's for surviving it. Nobody leaves empty-handed.

Intensity is measured twice — “how big does it feel right now?” before acknowledge, “and now?” at the close. The delta is the measure, not either number.
The wording is deliberate: never “how much better do you feel?” — that presupposes improvement and inflates a measure the evidence already distrusts (people reported feeling better after talking an upset through while showing no recovery at 3 days, 7 days or 2 months). The truer number is “did you actually do it?” at 24h.
The baseline fires before acknowledge because acknowledge is one of the things being measured. And it's not the same as the mid-flow “any better?”, which is a routing question asked while she's still working.
The human nudge fires on frequency only, never every session — usage volume is what predicts loneliness and dependence, so the right response to a high number is to send her away.
01

The options question — a menu, not a diagnosis

“there's no one right move here. does any of these sound like something you'd want to do?” — three concrete options in her own words, plus “show me some others” and an equally-legitimate “none of these feel possible right now.”

What changed and why: this was going to be a controllability assessment — decide whether her situation is fixable, then route her. That framing needed evidence we don't have (one cross-sectional study of 170 people, measuring an ability, about reappraisal rather than acceptance — and the largest test since found intensity dominates, not controllability).
So we stopped assessing and started asking. “What do you want to do?” needs no evidence at all — it's consent. Same screen, honest framing.
Why options rather than an open question: a woman who is low answers “no, nothing” to “can you do something about this?” every time — that's the low mood answering, not the situation. Options let her recognise a possibility instead of having to generate one. And “show me others” means “none of these” is a real preference rather than the only exit.
01b

Removed: the beat that told her not to trust her own read

Was: in the mixed lane, if she called it a swing, the moon “de-weighted” it — let's not give the peak the last word. Now: gone. She still sorts swing-or-real; we simply don't argue either way.

Why: zero evidence behind it, and a real cost — we may talk a woman out of an accurate read of someone who is genuinely pulling away, or genuinely unsafe. It also sat directly against the rule that the moon never overwrites her attributions about another person.
02

Every hold branch has a filler and the don’t-send guardrail

Was: only the 20-min branch had an activity or the “don’t act or send” line; no-time / 1–2 / 5-min issued a bare hold. Now: every branch gets a filler scaled to its length, and “don’t act on this or send anything yet” is verbatim on all of them.

Why the filler: Gottman — heart rate resets in ~20 min only with active distraction. Bushman — an empty delay is rehearsal. Those short branches are most sessions, so most sessions took the risk without the mitigation.
Why the guardrail everywhere: the entire point of a hold is that she doesn’t fire off the reactive thing — and 30 seconds is long enough to send a text. A hold without the guardrail protects nothing.
02e

Tired now changes the timing — and she keeps the choice

We asked about tiredness and then did nothing with it. Now: “you're tired. that usually makes it come out sharper than you mean.” → now · tomorrow · not sure.

You can't nap on demand, so tiredness shouldn't add a task — it should change the timing decision. Name the cost, don't decide for her, and keep “do it now anyway” a real un-shamed option. Tiredness happens at 2pm as well as 2am, so this isn't a night-only branch. Honest note: exhaustion-as-a-reason-to-wait is sensible, not evidenced — so we say it plainly and never dress it as science.
02f

“Want to try some other practices?” — an offer, then folded away entirely

Was: an escalation ladder. Then: an offer. Now: deleted as a beat — its five rungs simply are the activity list.

“Ladder” implies a hierarchy and, underneath it, that she's failing her way up it. It's also more honest: cold water, TIPP and the rest have no trial evidence, so offering beats prescribing.
02b

The body check acts now instead of writing a note

Was: a gap always became a Today action. Now: “can you eat something now?” → yes: go do it, the flow waits — it’s the most useful thing available in the moment. Only “not now” becomes a Today action.

Why: deferring food/water/rest deferred the one intervention that works immediately. The body check is also the beat with the strongest evidence in our whole flow — day-to-day sleep, stress and fatigue moved the regulation biomarker while cycle phase showed no population rhythm at all.
02c

The 10 modules collapse into the options — and each ends in a NOW thing

Was: she picked 3 options, then we routed her to a module — two choices for one decision. Now: the 3 options are the module entry points, phrased as her specific act. She picks an act, never a category name.

And with day-14 gone, no module may dead-end in insight. The four DRAFT modules (lonely, repair, real-hurt, work-clash) already produce a message she edits and sends. The six reframe modules now each attach a concrete act: sad → one small thing today · anxious → the specific fear + an anchor line · shame → repair-or-release, decided now · numb → one activation act · promotion → the actual sentence + what to gather · bias → the boundary line, rehearsed. Whatever the module produces becomes the if-then Today action.
02d

“Nothing feels possible” no longer exits empty-handed

Was: she picked “none feel possible”, got a warm line, and left — while every other path exited with an act and a plan. Now: honor it → warmth → one tiny comfort act → a coping if-then → door left open.

Why this mattered most: she is the group most likely to be low or hopeless; behavioural activation says the flat need an act most, not least; and leaving with nothing literally confirms her belief that nothing helps.
The resolution: separate acting on the situation (she declined — respected completely, never re-asked) from acting for herself (still available, requires solving nothing). Implementation intentions still apply — only the “then” changes from fixing to surviving: “if it lands on me again tonight, then I put the heat pack on and text mum.” She leaves with the same shape everyone else does.
The comfort act carries the weight here, not the warmth — self-compassion halves against an active comparison and app-delivered versions come out near-null, so it is tone, never a named method: never labelled, never explained, never claimed. (This panel said the opposite until 2026-07-25 — it called self-compassion “the one self-guided-evidenced move”, which is exactly the claim the spec forbids.) And repeated “none feel possible” across sessions is a signal to track gently — never something to confront her with in the moment.
03

The Today action is an if-then plan

Was: a today/wait chip — a timing choice. Now: if [trigger], then [what I say], stored as her action.

Why: Gollwitzer/Webb implementation intentions regulate affect at d = 0.53 against the fair comparator (goal intentions) — still the single biggest documented lever in the flow, previously reduced to a scheduling question. Corrected from 0.91, which is the figure against no-regulation instructions and overstates it. She fills both slots, not the model — self-generated intentions work better, and the model can't invent a person that way.
Where the act has an after (say-it-to-them, own-my-part, a-real-hurt, work-clash) it also gets the after-the-fight checklist. Nobody needs one for “have a shower.”
04

Crisis is always-on, every cycle phase

Never gated or weighted by cycle phase; errs toward firing when uncertain.

Why: suicide deaths +26% and attempts +17% at menstruation vs +13% admissions premenstrually. A luteal-weighted design would have relaxed exactly as risk peaks. A false positive costs one gentle question; a false negative costs everything.
05

The ~day-14 hold is gone — help her now

Was: “hold it until around day 14.” Now: act today once regulated; if she does want to wait, schedule the return.

Why: the day-14 number rested on a single n=23 study that never tested the contrast we were advising — and McGowan & Behar found a scheduled worry-window works while plain postponement fails. We had built the version that doesn’t work. The short 20-min delay stays; it’s well supported.
05b

Three acts reframed to match what the evidence actually says

Reach out → “tell someone who'll help you think it through”, not vent to someone. Talking through an upset produced no recovery at 3 days, 7 days or 2 months versus a control — yet people felt it helped. It only worked when the listener helped her think.
Self-compassion → demoted from a technique to tone. It beats doing nothing but roughly halves against an active comparison, and app versions come out near-null. The comfort act that follows does the real work, so the warmth lives in the writing instead of being a named method.
Repair → said frankly: apologising reliably helps the other person and the relationship. it might not make you feel better. and if you mean to and then don't, that tends to sit worse than never planning to. All three are supported, and the last one is worth knowing before she decides.

Also: “boundaries” and “grey rock” are out as terms — neither has trial evidence (grey rock has literally none). What's evidenced is the specific version: say no to this thing, don't reply tonight, leave at 6 like you planned.
06

Three copy lines stopped overclaiming

Naming: “research says… precise vs generic words” → “putting words to a feeling settles it down a bit.” Lieberman supports labeling; precise-vs-generic is granularity — textbook, not a citation we hold.
Breathing: keep the 4:6 count (4+6 = 10s = 6 breaths/min, exactly the supported pace); drop “fastest lever on the vagus nerve.”
Crying: permit, never prescribe — Gračanin: crying helps only when it leads to comfort; otherwise it’s rumination-crying.

07

The reflection beat never echoes her attributions

Reflect the event, the feeling, the body — never her read of what someone else meant. “he didn’t call, and you’ve been checking your phone” ✅ / “he’s clearly pulling away” ❌

Why: sycophancy risk is highest exactly at the reflection beat (Stanford/FAccT 2025 — models endorse distorted reads). This is the one place an AI can make a spiral worse while sounding kind.
