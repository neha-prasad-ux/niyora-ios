# What the evidence actually supports, act by act

Full review 2026-07-25 (six acts, primary sources checked, effect sizes read from
the papers rather than from abstracts). This is the honest version — several
findings contradict claims made earlier in this project, including mine.

---

## 🔴 THE FINDING THAT REFRAMES THE WHOLE FLOW

**Wenzel, Radtke, Kubiak & Rowland (2026), *Emotion*** — coordinated analysis of
**12 ambulatory datasets, N=1,511, 161,513 measurement occasions.** The largest
test of emotion-regulation strategy effectiveness that exists.

> **75–80% of the variance in strategy effectiveness is explained by the
> INTENSITY of the affective state being regulated — not by properties of the
> strategy.**

**Implication: the intensity gate is worth more than the entire act taxonomy.**
The controllability fork and the 12-act set are good design, but they are
second-order to something the flow already had. Rank accordingly.

Also from the same paper: acceptance, reappraisal and social sharing were on
average *favourable*; rumination and suppression were not. BUT there was
substantial between-person variability — strategies effective on average were
"ineffective or even counterproductive for a meaningful subset of individuals."
→ **no act may ever be presented as the right one.**

## ✅ THE BEST NEWS IN THE REVIEW — and it answers the self-guided worry

**Hagberg et al. (2023), *Internet Interventions*** — 3-arm RCT, **N=210**
(guided / **unguided** / waitlist), 8 weeks, 1-year follow-up:
- unguided vs waitlist: adaptive assertiveness **ES=1.00** post, **1.30 at 1yr**
- wellbeing **0.70 → 1.05**
- **unguided ≈ guided**
- and **aggressive assertiveness DECREASED** (ES −0.72 post, −0.90 follow-up) —
  the backlash/aggression worry is not supported by this trial

This is the strongest self-guided finding in the review and it lands on the act
that matters most. It is the best available answer to "does it work unguided on
a phone?"

| act | does it help? | self-guided version? |

### "Tell one person" — the uncomfortable one
Our low-effort on-ramp is the weakest-evidenced act in the set.
- **Zech & Rimé (2005):** talking about an emotional episode produced **no
  recovery at 3 days, 7 days, or 2 months** vs factual-description controls —
  **yet participants reported more subjective benefit.**
  → *a feature validated on self-report will look like it works when it doesn't.*
  This is precisely why "did you send it?" and 24h rumination are the right
  co-primaries for the user test.
- **Nils & Rimé (2012):** recovery occurred **exclusively** when the listener
  stimulated cognitive work. Empathic listening produced closeness, less
  loneliness, and *an impression of feeling better*.
- perceived support r=−0.35 vs **received** support r=−0.14 (93 studies).
  Bolger & Amarel: *visible* support "was either ineffective or exacerbated
  reactivity"; invisible support helped.
- co-rumination → depression is real but tiny (β≈.11, OR≈1.07), and **do not
  gender the risk** — women co-ruminate more (d≈−0.55) but the harm link is not
  reliably stronger in them.

→ **Design consequence:** the ladder's rung 3 should lean on **"look after myself
right now"** rather than "tell one person", and any reach-out should push toward
a listener who helps her think, not only sympathise.

## 🔴 A finding that changes the FOLLOW-UP LOOP

**Wang (2020), N=284:** people who **intended but failed** to apologise carried
the **most guilt and shame** — more than those who never intended to.
Also: Leunissen et al. (2014) — people **overestimate how aversive apologising
will be** (η²=.26), so the prompt is worth making.

→ Prompting a repair intention and letting it drop may leave her **worse than not
prompting at all.** The follow-up loop is therefore not just measurement, it is
**harm mitigation** for the repair act specifically. Do not ship repair prompts
without the follow-up.

## Cross-cutting design rules this review establishes

1. **Intensity, not act-choice, is the dominant variable** (75–80%). Get the gate
   right before optimising the menu.
2. **The delivery format is better evidenced than the scripts inside it.**
   Unguided ≈ guided; format didn't moderate detachment interventions; 53 RCTs of
   self-guided online ACT. **Ship the flow, don't over-claim the script.**
3. **Effects roughly halve against active controls** wherever anyone tested it
   (self-compassion 0.87→0.42; forgiveness 0.56→0.45; anxiety/self-criticism →
   ns). Set internal targets against an active comparator.
4. **Structured writing beats free writing** — directed questions r=.094 vs
   r=.011; ≥15 min r=.080 vs <15 min **r=−.007**. A short free-write is the
   *worst* configuration in the entire moderator set.
5. **Two named risks with real evidence:** unfinished repair (above), and
   unstructured problem-talk feeding brooding (experimentally confirmed). Both
   argue for **bounded, closed-loop actions rather than open invitations** —
   which is the structural argument for our flow over open chat.

## Four claims to stop making
1. DESC is evidence-based
2. grey rock / boundary-setting is a supported technique
3. soft-startup is causally established (and **never** the 94% figure)
4. apologising will make her feel better
